## Why you are doing this

In this course, you will have choices about what you build, which resources you use, and how you practice. A learning contract turns those choices into a focused plan.

Your contract is not a promise to predict everything that will happen. It is a starting agreement between you and your instructor about:

- the competency you intend to develop,
- the work you will do to practice it,
- the evidence you will produce, and
- the standard you will use to decide whether you are ready to be assessed.

You may revise your contract when you learn more. Tell your instructor what changed and why.

## What makes a strong contract?

A strong contract is:

- **Specific:** It names an observable skill, not just a broad topic.
- **Relevant:** It connects to your project, interests, or professional goals.
- **Challenging but possible:** It stretches you without requiring an unmanageable project.
- **Evidence-based:** It explains what you will show, not only what you will read or watch.
- **Time-bounded:** It identifies a first milestone and a target assessment date.

“I will learn APIs” is too broad. “I will retrieve data with `fetch`, display loading and error states, and explain how the response is transformed before rendering” is specific enough to plan and assess.

## Step 1: Choose a competency

Review the [WDD 231 Competency Guide](../resources/competencies/) before choosing. It explains what each competency includes, gives examples of observable practice, and describes what proficiency means at the course level.

Choose one primary competency from the guide, or propose an equivalent competency for approval.

The competency guide includes details for each of these areas:

- Semantic and accessible HTML
- Responsive CSS and inclusive visual design
- JavaScript, DOM manipulation, and events
- Data-driven rendering and modules
- APIs and asynchronous programming
- Application state, navigation, and interaction
- SEO
- Collaboration and technical communication
- Optional: Performance and deployment

The guide also identifies two cross-cutting practices that should appear in
every contract when relevant:

- Debugging and evidence-based troubleshooting
- Responsible AI-assisted development

You may include supporting competencies, but choose only one primary competency for this contract. Cross-cutting practices are not counted as the primary competency; include them when they naturally occur in your work.

## Step 2: Check your starting point

Briefly describe what you can already do and what is still uncertain.

Consider:

- What have you built that is related to this competency?
- What part can you explain confidently?
- What part do you currently need help with?
- What evidence would show meaningful growth?

## Step 3: Write your contract

Copy the template below into your submission and complete every section. Use concrete language and link to resources when possible.

```md
# Learning Contract: [Your competency]

Student: [Your name]
Date: [Date]
Target assessment date: [Date]

## Primary competency

By the end of this contract, I will be able to:

[Write an observable outcome. Begin with “I can...”]

## My starting point

I already know/can:

[Describe your current ability and include a small example if useful.]

I still need to learn or improve:

[Name the specific gaps or questions you have.]

## My learning plan

I will use these resources:

- [Resource, link, or person]
- [Resource, link, or person]

I will practice by:

[Describe the feature, experiment, exercise, or project work you will complete.]

My first milestone, due [date], is:

[Describe the smallest meaningful increment you will complete.]

## Evidence of learning

I will submit or demonstrate:

- [Working implementation or other artifact]
- [Short explanation of how it works]
- [One test, experiment, or verification result]
- [One bug, challenge, or design decision and what I learned from it]

I will know I am ready when:

[List two or three observable success criteria.]

## Support and risks

The help I may need is:

[Instructor conference, peer feedback, documentation, tutoring, or another support.]

A risk to my plan is:

[Name a likely obstacle and how you will respond.]

## AI use, if applicable

I expect to use AI for:

[No AI / explanations / brainstorming / debugging / code suggestions.]

I will verify AI suggestions by:

[Describe the documentation, tests, experiments, or review you will use.]
```

## Step 4: Review your scope

Before submitting, check your plan against these questions:

- Could another person tell whether I met my outcome?
- Is my first milestone small enough to finish on time?
- Does my evidence demonstrate understanding, not just a finished feature?
- Have I included a way to test or verify my work?
- If my plan fails, do I know what I will try next?

If the answer to any question is no, revise the contract before submitting it.

## Example contract

```md
# Learning Contract: APIs and asynchronous programming

Student: Jordan Lee
Date: September 9
Target assessment date: September 23

## Primary competency

By the end of this contract, I can retrieve data from an external API,
render the results in the DOM, and handle loading, empty, and error states.

## My starting point

I can use events and functions, but I am not yet confident about async/await,
reading a response object, or deciding where error handling belongs.

## My learning plan

I will review the Fetch Basics module and the API documentation for my project.
I will build a small search feature before integrating it into my main project.

My first milestone is a button that makes one request and displays either the
returned name or a visible error message.

## Evidence of learning

I will demonstrate the feature, explain the request and response flow, show a
network request in browser developer tools, and document one bug involving an
unexpected response shape and how I fixed it.

I will know I am ready when I can explain the code without reading a script and
can modify it to handle an empty result.
```

## Step 5: Submit and discuss

Submit your completed contract according to your instructor’s directions. Be prepared to discuss:

1. What you are trying to learn
2. Why your plan is appropriate
3. What evidence will demonstrate mastery
4. Where you are most likely to get stuck

Your instructor may approve the contract, suggest a smaller or larger scope, or ask you to clarify the evidence. Approval is part of the learning process; it is not a judgment that your first draft must be perfect.

## After approval

Use the contract during your work. If your project or plan changes, record:

- what changed,
- why it changed,
- what competency the new work supports, and
- whether your evidence still proves the original outcome.

At the end, compare your contract with your results. Explain what you learned, which success criteria you met, and what you would pursue next.
