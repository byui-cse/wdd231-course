# Peer Contract Review

## Purpose

A peer contract review is a two-way conversation about the slices in one learning contract. You explain your work, your partner asks questions, and together you find gaps and connections. Your peer does not certify anything. The review prepares you for the [instructor or TA competency pass-off](./competency-pass-off-new).

> Record each review in your [Competency Progress Tracker](../competency-progress-tracker.md).

## This is a conversation, not a presentation

The review works best when both people are actively thinking. Your role as reviewer depends on how well you know the material.

**If you know the slices well (higher mastery):**

- Ask questions that help your partner connect the dots, not questions that give away the answer.
- Ask "why?" and "what would happen if...?" instead of correcting.
- Let your partner finish thinking before you add anything.

**If you are newer to the slices (lower mastery):**

- Keep asking questions until you understand it yourself.
- Ask your partner to show it again, use a different example, or explain a term.
- Your confusion is useful. If your partner cannot explain it simply, that points to a gap worth reviewing.

Either way, both of you should leave understanding the slices better than when you started. If only one person is talking, change the format: ask a question or switch roles.

## Before you meet

- [ ] List the slices in your contract.
- [ ] Make sure your work runs and you can show it.
- [ ] Gather at least one piece of evidence for each slice (a test, screenshot, inspection result, or note).
- [ ] Fill in the top of the [review record](#review-record).

Do not memorize a script. You should understand the work well enough to explain it in your own words.

## The conversation

Work through these steps for every slice in the contract.

1. **State the goal.** What did you intend to learn or be able to do, and how does it connect to your project?
2. **Show it working.** Demonstrate a normal case and, when relevant, an edge case (empty data, invalid input, narrow viewport, failed request).
3. **Explain the learning.** What was difficult or surprising? What did you try first? What changed after testing or feedback?
4. **Ask and answer questions.** The reviewer chooses questions from the [question bank](#question-bank). Follow up on answers, and swap roles if the reviewer has something to demonstrate too.
5. **Summarize.** Write down one thing to review and one thing you are ready to demonstrate at the pass-off.

## Question bank

Choose a few questions that fit the slice. You do not need to ask them all. Follow-up questions based on what you hear are often better than these.

### Any slice

- What were you trying to learn or improve?
- What part is hardest to explain?
- What choice had more than one reasonable option?
- What evidence shows your solution works?
- What did you try when you got stuck?
- What would break first if the requirements changed?

- If you used AI, what did it suggest, what did you reject, and how did you verify the result?

## Tips

**Explaining:** start with the goal before opening the code, use real examples, and say what you do not know. A clear explanation of one slice beats a rushed tour of the whole project.

**Reviewing:** ask to understand, not to prove you know more. Give your partner time to think. Point to specific evidence when giving feedback, and end with one strength and one suggested next step.

## Review record

Copy this into your notes or tracker.

```md

Date: [Date]
Peer conversation partner: [Name]
Slices reviewed: [List each slice]

1. What I demonstrated across the contract:

2. Evidence I showed:

- [Artifact]
- [Test or verification]
- [Explanation, challenge, or decision]

3. What my peer helped me notice:

[Gaps, challenges, or connections discovered in the conversation]

4. What I learned by asking questions or listening:

[Something that became clearer for you as the reviewer]

5. Review status: [Every slice reviewed / More review needed]

6. My next step:

[Specific next action]
```

## You are done when

- every slice in the contract has been discussed,
- you have evidence or a clear explanation for each slice,
- both partners have asked and answered questions,
- gaps and next steps are recorded, and
- your Competency Progress Tracker is updated.

When every slice of a competency has been reviewed, request the [competency pass-off](./competency-pass-off-new).
